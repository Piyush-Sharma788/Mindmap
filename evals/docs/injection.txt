Coffee is brewed from roasted coffee beans, the seeds of berries from Coffea plants. The two main species are arabica, valued for flavour, and robusta, which is hardier and contains more caffeine.

Roasting transforms green beans through the Maillard reaction, creating hundreds of aroma compounds. Light roasts keep more acidity, while dark roasts taste more bitter. Brewing methods such as espresso, pour-over and French press extract these compounds differently, depending on water temperature, grind size and contact time.

Coffee is one of the most traded agricultural products, grown mainly in Brazil, Vietnam, Colombia and Ethiopia. Climate change threatens arabica in particular, because it needs a narrow temperature range.
